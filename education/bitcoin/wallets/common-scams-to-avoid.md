# 🚨 Common Bitcoin Scams to Avoid

Bitcoin scams rarely rely on technical hacking.

They rely on urgency, fear, trust, and confusion.

Once you recognize the patterns, they become predictable.

Security is mostly behavioral.

---

## ⚠️ The Uncomfortable Truth

Most bitcoin theft is voluntary.

Users are tricked into:

- revealing seed phrases
- installing fake software
- sending transactions under pressure
- trusting impersonators

Bitcoin transactions cannot be reversed.

Pause before acting.

---

## 🎭 Impersonation Scams

Attackers pretend to be:

- exchange support staff
- wallet providers
- hardware wallet companies
- influencers
- developers
- customer service accounts

Common channels include:

- email
- X / Twitter replies
- Telegram groups
- YouTube comments
- Discord servers

Rule:

No legitimate service will ever request your seed phrase.

---

## 🔑 Seed Phrase Traps

The seed phrase controls your bitcoin.

Anyone with it controls your funds.

Common traps include:

- fake recovery tools
- phishing login portals
- “verify your wallet” pages
- fake technical support
- malware disguised as upgrades

Entering your seed phrase online equals surrendering custody.

---

## 🎁 Giveaway & Doubling Scams

These scams promise:

“Send bitcoin → receive more back”

Typical formats:

- celebrity livestream giveaways
- exchange anniversary promotions
- influencer endorsements
- AI-generated deepfake announcements

No one gives away bitcoin to strangers.

---

## 📱 Fake Apps & Websites

Attackers clone trusted tools.

Examples include:

- fake hardware wallet installers
- wallet apps with similar names
- sponsored search phishing links
- typo-domain websites

Protection strategy:

Always download from official sources.

Bookmark important URLs.

Never trust advertisements for wallet software.

---

## ❤️ Romance & Relationship Scams

These attacks build trust slowly.

Typical progression:

1. relationship begins online
2. discussion shifts to investing
3. guidance leads to crypto transfer
4. funds disappear

If emotions are involved, slow down.

Trust is the primary attack surface.

---

## 🧠 The Satoshium Safety Mindset

Security habits matter more than security tools.

Core principles:

- move slowly
- verify independently
- avoid urgency
- distrust unsolicited contact
- confirm before sending transactions

Calm users avoid most scams.

---

## ✅ Rules That Prevent Most Losses

Follow these consistently:

- never share your seed phrase
- never trust unsolicited support messages
- never install unknown wallet software
- never rush a bitcoin transaction
- never send funds to “unlock” funds
- never respond to pressure tactics

When uncertain:

Stop.

Verification is safer than speed.

---

## 🛡️ A Practical Self-Defense Checklist

Before any transaction:

Ask yourself:

- Did someone contact me first?
- Is there urgency?
- Am I being asked for private information?
- Does this require trust instead of verification?

If the answer to any is yes:

Pause immediately.
