# ⛏️ Block Reward & Halving

Bitcoin mining is not just computation.

It is the mechanism that distributes new bitcoin, secures the network, and enforces Bitcoin’s fixed monetary policy.

Miners earn revenue through the **block reward**, which includes both newly issued bitcoin and transaction fees.

---

## 🔎 The Simple Explanation

Roughly every 10 minutes, a new Bitcoin block is created.

The miner who produces that block receives:

- a block subsidy (new bitcoin)
- transaction fees (paid by users)

Over time, the subsidy decreases while fees become more important.

This transition is intentional.

---

## 🧱 What the Block Reward Includes

The term “block reward” contains two parts:

**Block subsidy**

New bitcoin created according to the issuance schedule

**Transaction fees**

Payments from users competing for limited block space

Only the subsidy halves.

Fees do not.

A useful mental model:

Subsidy is the mint  
Fees are the market

---

## ⚖️ What a Halving Is (and Why It Exists)

Approximately every four years, the block subsidy is cut in half.

This process is called the **halving**.

Halvings:

- reduce new supply entering circulation
- reinforce long-term scarcity
- eliminate discretionary monetary expansion
- maintain predictable issuance

Bitcoin’s monetary policy is enforced by rules, not committees.

---

## 📉 The Issuance Schedule

Bitcoin’s supply decreases over time according to a fixed schedule.

The long-term progression:

Early era

Subsidy dominates miner income

Middle era

Subsidy declines, fees increase in importance

Long-term era

Fees become the primary miner incentive

Bitcoin does not rely on permanent inflation.

---

## 💸 Fees: The Long-Term Incentive Model

Transaction fees reflect demand for block space.

As usage grows:

Higher demand → higher fees  
Higher fees → stronger miner incentives  
Stronger incentives → stronger network security

Fees form the long-term security foundation of Bitcoin.

---

## 🛡️ Security Budget

Miner revenue funds proof-of-work security.

This total revenue is called the **security budget**.

Today:

Security budget = subsidy + fees

Future:

Security budget = mostly fees

Healthy fee markets indicate real demand for settlement.

---

## ⚠️ Common Misunderstandings

**“Halvings stop mining”**

Mining continues. Difficulty adjusts as the market responds.

**“Fees will always be negligible”**

Fees increase when demand for block space increases.

**“Halvings guarantee price increases”**

Halvings affect supply issuance, not market demand.

**“Bitcoin eventually runs out”**

Issuance declines, but miners continue earning transaction fees.

---

## ❓ Quick FAQ

**What exactly halves?**

Only the block subsidy halves.

**Do halvings change block timing?**

No. Difficulty adjustments maintain the ~10-minute average.

**Will miners still be paid in the future?**

Yes. Fees are the long-term incentive mechanism.

**Why not issue bitcoin forever?**

Bitcoin’s fixed supply creates predictable scarcity and monetary neutrality.
