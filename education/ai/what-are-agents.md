# 🤖 AI Agents — Explained

AI agents are software systems that can observe information, make decisions, and take action toward a goal.

Instead of only answering prompts, agents can follow multi-step workflows, use tools, and operate with a degree of autonomy.

---

## 🔎 The Simple Definition

**An AI agent is software that can perceive a task, reason about what to do next, and take action to move toward an objective.**

A chatbot answers.  
An agent acts.

---

## ⚙️ What Makes an Agent Different

Agents differ from traditional AI interfaces in several important ways:

- **Goals** — they are trying to accomplish something across steps
- **Memory** — they may retain context between actions
- **Reasoning** — they evaluate options before acting
- **Tools** — they can call APIs, access files, or interact with systems
- **Autonomy** — they can continue working without constant prompting

Together, these capabilities allow agents to participate in structured workflows rather than single responses.

---

## 🧠 How AI Agents Work

Most agent systems operate through a repeating cycle:

1. **Input** — receive a task or environment signal
2. **Interpretation** — determine what matters
3. **Planning** — select steps or tools
4. **Execution** — perform actions
5. **Feedback** — evaluate results and adapt

Stronger agents combine models, rules, memory, and external infrastructure.

---

## 🛠️ What Agents Can Be Used For

Agents support a wide range of structured activities:

- research and information gathering
- workflow automation
- monitoring systems and responding to events
- drafting documents or code
- structured decision support

As their capabilities grow, so does the importance of governance.

---

## ⚠️ Why Agent Safety Matters

Agents are more powerful than prompt-response systems because they take action.

That makes constraints essential.

Safe agent systems should ensure:

- explicit operational boundaries
- inspectable execution steps
- rule-constrained decision pathways
- intervention mechanisms when risks appear

Autonomy without guardrails creates instability.

---

## 🧭 Why Satoshium Cares About Agents

Agents are where intelligence meets responsibility.

If agents can plan, act, and affect real systems, then their behavior should be:

- **Verifiable** — actions can be inspected
- **Governed** — rules define acceptable behavior
- **Auditable** — decisions can be reviewed later
- **Aligned** — outputs remain anchored to shared definitions

Satoshium explores how agents operate inside structured trust environments rather than unrestricted autonomy.

The goal is not maximum independence.

**The goal is reliable, rule-bound intelligence.**
